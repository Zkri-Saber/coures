Right now your page only exists on your computer. GitHub Pages can put it on the internet for free, with a real address you can share.

## What you need

- A free account at [github.com](https://github.com)
- Your `index.html`, `style.css` and any images, in one folder

## Steps

1. **Create a repository.** On GitHub, click **New** (or the **+** menu, then **New repository**). Name it something like `about-me`, choose **Public**, and click **Create repository**.
2. **Upload your files.** On the new repository page, click **uploading an existing file**. Drag in `index.html`, `style.css` and your images, then click **Commit changes**.
3. **Turn on Pages.** Go to **Settings**, then **Pages** in the left menu. Under **Build and deployment**, set **Source** to **Deploy from a branch**, choose the `main` branch and the `/ (root)` folder, and click **Save**.
4. **Wait a minute, then visit.** Your page will be at `https://YOUR-USERNAME.github.io/about-me/`. Refresh the Pages settings screen to see the link.

## Making changes

Edit a file on your computer and upload it again, or click a file on GitHub, then the pencil icon to edit it right in the browser. Each time you commit, GitHub Pages republishes your site within a minute or two.

> Your home page must be called `index.html` (all lowercase). That's the file browsers open when someone visits your address.

## Before you share

- Everything in a public repository can be seen by anyone. Don't upload personal information like your address or phone number.
- Check your page on a phone as well as a computer.

:::quiz
Q: What must your home page file be called?
- [ ] `home.html`
- [x] `index.html`
- [ ] `Index.HTML`
Why: Web servers look for `index.html` by default, and GitHub Pages is case-sensitive, so use lowercase.

Q: Where do you turn GitHub Pages on?
- [ ] In your account profile
- [x] In the repository's Settings, under Pages
- [ ] It turns on automatically for every repository
Why: Pages is switched on per repository, in Settings, then Pages.
:::
