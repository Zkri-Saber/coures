Now it's your turn. In this project you'll refresh your resume for a **real job posting**, putting everything from the lesson into practice, and submit it for review. You'll get specific, constructive feedback to help you make it even stronger.

## What you'll create

1. A **tailored resume** (PDF or Word), one to two pages
2. The **job posting** you tailored it for (a link, or the text pasted into a document)
3. A short **note** of 3 to 5 sentences explaining the main changes you made and why

## Steps

### 1. Choose your target job

Find a real job, internship, volunteer role or university programme you'd genuinely like to apply for. Save the posting: postings are often taken down once the role is filled.

### 2. Make a keyword list

Highlight the skills, tools and qualities the posting asks for. Pick the 8 to 10 that matter most and next to each one, write where you have **proof** of it.

### 3. Update your master resume

Add anything missing from your experience: recent jobs, projects, volunteering, courses, certificates. Rewrite weak bullets as achievements:

```text
Action verb  +  what you did  +  the result
```

Add numbers wherever you honestly can.

### 4. Tailor a copy for this job

- Write a 2 to 3 line summary aimed at this role.
- Put the most relevant experience and bullets first.
- Use the posting's language for skills you really have.
- Show at least **two transferable skills** with evidence.
- Cut anything that doesn't help for this application.

### 5. Check it works for software

- One column, standard headings, no tables, text boxes or images with text
- Contact details in the main body, not the page header
- Consistent date format throughout
- Do the **plain-text paste test** and fix anything that comes out jumbled

### 6. Polish

Proofread it, read it aloud, then ask a friend or family member to read it too. If you used an AI tool, check every suggestion against the truth and make sure it still sounds like you.

### 7. Save and submit

Save the file as `Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf` (or `.docx`) and submit it with the job posting and your note.

> **Privacy:** you may remove your phone number and street address from the copy you submit for review. Keep your name, city and email.

## Requirements

Your reviewer will check your resume against these requirements. Make sure you meet every one before you submit.

| Area | Your resume meets expectations when... |
| --- | --- |
| Targeting | It's clearly tailored to the chosen posting: the summary, skills and order of content match the role |
| Structure | It uses standard sections in a logical order: contact details, summary, experience and/or projects, education, skills |
| Achievements | Most bullets start with an action verb and describe results, with numbers where possible |
| Transferable skills | At least two transferable skills are shown with evidence, not just listed |
| Keywords | Important words from the posting appear naturally, and every claim is accurate |
| Readability | Single column, no tables or text boxes, consistent dates, and a clean result in the plain-text test |
| Polish | No spelling or grammar mistakes, consistent formatting, one to two pages, sensible file name |

## Self-check before submitting

- [ ] I chose a real posting and included it with my submission
- [ ] My summary is written for this role
- [ ] Every bullet is true, and I could talk about it in an interview
- [ ] I've shown at least two transferable skills with evidence
- [ ] My resume passed the plain-text paste test
- [ ] Someone else has proofread it
- [ ] My note explains what I changed and why

## After you submit

You'll receive feedback on what's working and what to improve. Read it carefully, update your resume, and keep the improved version as your new master copy. A resume is never really "finished": refresh it every time you gain a new skill or experience.
